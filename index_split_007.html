This document uses the Steele Sermon / Ferrara sequence, as this seems to yield the most meaningful results.

The Pymander addresses Polar Opposite pairs of cards for the seven Governors, but there is another secret key within the numbering of the Tarot Majors that also addresses pairs. To discover this secret, we can start by matching known Governor pairs, and then noticing the pattern in the remainder. Naturally, the fit is not perfect, as other pairings can be recognised, and presumably were so recognised in the development of the sequence.

However, we must use the traditional numbering sequence (e.g. Marseille), rather than that espoused by The Golden Dawn in which Strength and Justice were swapped.

What follows is a sequence of operations that show how to work the Steele sermon / Ferrara sequence to remove obfuscations and reveal the hidden meaning.

Noting the Wheel of Fate and the World as outliers, it is a simple matter to recognise them as a Duality pair of polar opposites, representing “as above, so below”. The main hint in the RWS deck is the four elements in the corners of both cards, with the Wheel versions reading books, to indicate the abstract, conceptual nature. Therefore, we can move them to a pair at the bottom.

We now have Justice/Hanged Man as Jupiter. The Hermit/Strength pair seems meaningful, but not quite Polar Opposite. Strength is Ops, nature Goddess, and the Hermit is Saturn, her husband. There are also numerous Alchemical pictures showing an alchemist, looking much like a Hermit, needing a lantern, glasses and a walking stick in order to hope to follow an understand the footsteps of Mother Nature. This is Saturn, in his quest for knowledge, trying to understand the mystery of his wife Ops, which is reflected in Alchemy and later Science trying to understand the natural world around us.

Note that the Chariot, Temperance and Justice cards are now grouped together, and all three are mentioned in Plato’s Phaedrus. Although the Chariot is chosen to represent Mercury in this document, it is recognised that the connection is a little weak. We also have Hermes (the Greek Mercury) as the Fool, but we keep the two separate, as one is clearly human, and the other a governor. Mercury is generally known as a trickster or liar in his negative aspect, which may imply that he is represented by the Father of Lies, the Devil. The choice is unclear at present, so the cards are left as is. The reader may of course choose to think otherwise.
