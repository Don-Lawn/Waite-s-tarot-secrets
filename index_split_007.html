The Seven Governors

The seven Governors are not addressed individually in the Pymander, presumably because they are assumed to be so well-known to readers that further explanation is unnecessary. Their generation, on the other hand, is described:

[P13]       … The Demiurge made the seven planetary Governors…

Identification of the seven cards representing the Governors is crucial to interpreting the Major Arcana according to the Pymander.

The Mortal Life

As a consequence of this division into sexes, males and females also become mortal, and the old die to make way for the young. Thus, Death is a consequence of Love. The card [Death] represents this new mortality. But within Death we are reminded that the spirit and soul remain immortal.

[P38]       God said “Go forth and multiply. Let those with a Mind know themselves to be immortal, and that the cause of Death [Death] is the love of the body, and let him learn all things that are [Hermit]”.

Judgement

With the Death of the body comes the release of the spirit and mind to return to heaven. This is described from [P4], and represented in [Judgement], which is often interpreted according to the Christian mythology of Judgement Day when the dead rise up from the grave to be judged.

Plato’s Chariot

The meaning of several major cards – Chariot, Temperance and Justice – are not clearly identifiable in the Pymander, but instead are described in Plato’s Chariot Allegory, which in turn is found within Phaedrus. While this may seem like an inconsistency it can also be seen as confirmation that the Pymander, and the Major Arcana, are neo-platonic in nature. Interpreting these three cards according to the original Plato results in consistent interpretation with the Pymander

The full relevant text from Phaedrus, from English translation by Jowett, 1892, is given in Appendix C

Gods, Governors, and Secrets

In the following, the term Governor comes from the Pymander, and may be taken to mean the Roman Gods of the seven classical planets. It is interesting to note that the Pymander recognizes only the one God above all, and so refers to the planets as Governors rather than as Gods.

In the Major Arcana, the Roman Gods are rarely represented directly. This is likely deliberate to obscure the true meaning of the cards. This could be for either or both of two reasons. Firstly, these “secrets” were considered to be of great power, and not to be divulged to the masses. Secondly, anything of a pagan nature had to be kept from the eyes of the Catholic Church.

Oral tradition has it that the Major Arcana is a pictorial representation of a deep secret which had to be represented in pictures rather than written down, so that the secret could be passed using the cards, but accompanied by oral training. Given the coincidental timing of the emergence of the Pymander and the Tarot in Renaissance Italy, it seems likely that this secret is the Pymander itself. This book and matching deck assume that to be the case, and reconstruct the original meaning of the cards accordingly.

Historically, Pope Sixtus IV (1471 - 1484) accepted the Pymander as a valid addition to Christian lore, but his eventual successor Pope Clement VIII (1592 - 1605) reversed this decision in 1599, declaring it to be heretical.

Interpretation of Inverted cards

When a card appears inverted, consider its astrological influence. The card itself indicates the presence of the influence in the reading context, but we must consider that the querent may be either the source or the recipient of that influence.
In RWS, some cards appear to be negative when upright, and so positive when inverted. The reader may of course choose their own method of interpreting the reversal polarity of the card.

Also note that there may be different aspects to a decan, and often some aspects are not elaborated in the card imagery. Ultimately, the ideal is for the reader to be familiar with the full astrology of each card.

For the Pymander interpretations given, the interpretations focus on the direction of action of the influence.

Upright cards are considered to be the querent acting as a result of the decan’s influence, whereas inverted cards represent the querent being subjected to the influence via some other person or situation.
