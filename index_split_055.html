Pip Cards – Theory

Astrology

The Tarot was created during the Renaissance, but much of the detail of the Minor Arcana comes from the Golden Dawn, but this appears to still be based on the Seven Governors, not including Neptune, Uranus, Pluto, Ceres, etc. This book therefore uses the same system. Provided below are some brief descriptions of astrological knowledge as used here.

The Seven Governors

Saturn, the Governor of Knowledge

“Knowledge is Power”

Saturn has wisdom, which is the pursuit of knowledge. Yet, his pursuit of knowledge was such that it overshadowed, and eliminated, compassion. He was a dictatorial ruler who largely treated humans as little more than pets. He ruled during the Golden Age, which equates to the time within the Garden of Eden. He mistreated his son Jupiter, resulting in a war between the Gods and humanity. Also associated with gardening. His wife Ops was goddess of Nature and crops. His Chariot is drawn by dragons, which are predator beasts with power and intellect, but no compassion. Generally, a negative influence, except for wisdom/intelligence.

Jupiter, the King of Abundance

“It’s good to be the King”

Jupiter lived to the fullest. Associated with abundance, revelry and transformation He is a party animal. Known for largess. However, in return for human cooperation in the war between the Gods, he granted humans self-rule. A positive influence.

Mars, the God of War

“Do what I say or else…”

Mars , God of War, embodies ambition and aggression, wants everything his own way, and uses violence to achieve that. Also known for Hubris, which in modern language means arrogance, but in the times of the Renaissance, also had connotations of enjoying the degradation of others (old meaning of Hubris). Arrogant, domineering. A negative influence.

Apollo, the charioteer of the Sun

“All things bright and beautiful”

Apollo drives the Sun across the skies during the day, using his Chariot with four horses. The Sun provides the energy that powers everything. Wonderful, magnificent, brilliant, glowing, outflow. A definite positive influence, but with a tendency towards excessive outflow and narcissism.

Venus, the Goddess of Love

“I just want to be loved”

Goddess of feminine sexuality, including motherhood. Known for dalliance with other Gods besides her spouse, much as was Jupiter.

Mercury, the Social Genius

“Clever Clogs”, “Trickster”

Known for intelligence, music, envy, conning his fellow gods out of their possessions. Sort of a cross between Jupiter and Saturn. He has Jupiter’s self-indulgence, and Saturn’s selfishness. He gets his way be being smarter than the other Gods. A trickster. Whilst Saturn is a stern, serious and dedicated collector of knowledge, Mercury relies more on natural talent, dealing with issues as they arise “on the wing”.

Luna, the Goddess or Variation

“Ebb and Flow”

The Moon increases and decreases. In her positive aspect she represents increase of good things, and decrease of bad things, but also the reverse. Generally seen as a positive influence, and feminine (Luna) due to her control over menstrual and other breeding cycles.

Signs

Note that in the following, a sign can cover both positive and negative aspects of their area of control. For example, Tauruss about both wealth and poverty. Also, a sign can be considered to be about “the need for” their area of control, e.g., Libras about the need for balance, more than having balance. Ariess about the need to express their will upon the world. Whether people are successful or not is another matter, as both polarities are covered by the sign.

 

The First Six Signs: Individual Focus

These first six signs address the growth and development from child to senescence, from a personal point of view.

Aries, the Child through to Marriage

Ruled by Mars, representing violence, ambition and wilfulness. About the self, and the Will. Symbolised by a head-butting ram.

Taurus, the Parent, from Marriage to Empty nester

Ruled by Venus, representing love and motherhood. All about material comforts, domestic bliss, wealth, etc. Symbolised by a bull, who is happy with his cows, and very protective of them.

Gemini, the Social aspect of Life

Ruled by Mercury, representing socialisation. All about communication and social interaction. Symbolised by the twins Castor and Pollux who were punished by being made to travel eternally between Earth and heaven, but out of sync with each other, so they only ever see each other momentarily as they pass. Hence the need for communication.

Cancer, the Home aspect of Life

Ruled by the Moon, representing variability and mood. Emotional, changeable, intuitive. Symbolised by the Crab. Soft on the inside, hard on the outside. Very defensive. Concerned with creating an emotionally safe home environment.

Leo, the search for Purpose and Glory

Ruled by the Sun, representing outflow and glory. The need to be magnificent, and to be admired and appreciated by all. Proud. What you are naturally good at, and drawn to, what you should be doing What makes you happy. What made you happy as a child, before the material world corrupted you. Symbolised by a Lion.

Virgo, the Sunset Years

Ruled by Mercury, the use of wit and cunning to mould one’s environment. Old souls. Conscientious, clever, witty. Symbolised by the virgin Astrea, who uses the scales of Libran the pursuit of Justice. She gave up on humans during the bronze age, and ascended to become the constellation.

We could say that this is Mercury after he has matured, and stopped being a prat.

The Last Six Signs: Societal Harmony

The next six signs address societal issues, starting with the basic awareness of others, through position in society, to a withdrawal from society. The first six were about the individual, but the second six are more about how a society made up of individuals can get along. They tend to mirror or repeat the concerns of the first six.

Libra, recognising the needs of Self and Others

Ruled by Venus, representing the beginnings of consideration of others. The need for balance, harmony, fairness and Justice. Libras the scales used by Astraea in the assessment of Justice. However, Librans may struggle with achieving balance. Librans are generally aiming for a pleasant harmonious life.

Libras about how individuals can coexist, through fairness and Justice. It also has reference to the marriage of opposites, as in the Caduceus.

Scorpio, managing and resisting the Dark Side of human nature

Ruled by Mars, representing the tension between the needs of self, and the needs to cooperate. Passion, emotions, including dark emotions. Symbolised by the scorpion, but in olden days also by the eagle.

Scorpio seems to be about how to deal with strong, deeply rooted emotions within society, such as Sex and Death.

Sagittarius, the Idealist, string to improve life for all

Ruled by Jupiter, representing social Justice. Concerned with self-rule of the people, by the people, for the people. The opposite of dictatorship. Symbolised by a Centaur Archer (Chiron). This can be generalised to all forms of philosophy.

Capricorn, Leading, Providing for, and Protecting the Family

Ruled by Saturn, representing the tension between egotistical self-interest, and the love of family, and addresses Saturn’s ego. How is status and position on the ladder of society determined? Money is highly significant in this. Symbolised by the half-goat, half fish.

Aquarius, the search for Understanding this World, and Beyond

Ruled by Saturn, representing the pursuit of knowledge. It is symbolised by Enki who invented carrying Water to crops. Saturn’s knowledge will dominate the coming Age of Aquarius, but beware the egotism of the Saturnian nature.

 

Pisces, the search for Contentment within Self

Ruled by Jupiter, representing comfort and contentment. Concerned with a more mature version of Jupiter, once he has given up sowing his wild oats, and has settled into a calmer and more relaxed lifestyle. Possibly the secrets to a happy life.

Decans

Each card traditionally represents one decan or decanate of the zodiac, except for the aces. A decan is 10 degrees of the zodiac, and three decans make up each sign.

● 2, 3, 4 represent Cardinal signs.
● 5, 6, 7 represent Fixed signs
● 8,9,10 represent Mutable signs.

While the normal 12 signs of the zodiac are found around the path of the milky way, there are also signs for each of the decans, which can be found further from this equatorial region, more towards the poles of the sky.

In observation of people, it seems apparent that the decans sometimes apply outside of their 10-degree range, and can often be found applying to any individuals from the relevant sign. For example, the need for Freedom of Gemini can often be found present in Gemini also.

Rulership of decans in Renaissance astrology is assigned to governors on a rotating basis, in the order Saturn, Jupiter Mars, Sun, Venus, Mercury, Moon, starting with Mars ruling Aries-one at Wands.

Apprentice, Journeyman, Master

Within the three decans of each sign, there appears a pattern. The first decan seems to be about learning, the second about doing, and the third about mastery. This is reminiscent of the titles in a trade system.

  • The Apprentice learns the rules
  • The Journeyman applies the rules
  • The Master sets the rules.

These titles are used for each decan throughout this document, as a memory aid for students.

Consider this pattern within two opposite signs, Aries and Libra

In Aries, the issue is learning to deal with life, in general. In the first decan, the child is learning about life. They seem naïve, and may be prone to tantrums as the only mechanism for dealing with unpleasantness. Then, as a teenager, he starts to explore and adventure, living his own life. Then, hopefully, he has mastered the learning phase and moves into the last decan, which is maturity, marriage and family. The tradesman pattern of Ariess compressed into the first twenty years or so, but the pattern is still there

In Libra, the first decan are the perfectionists. They are learning about social interaction, but are not competent, and so may suffer. As a result, they may turn their attention to physical, mechanical things that they can control, transferring this unhappiness of the social scene. In the second decan, these Librans have learned the rules of social manipulation, and apply them routinely. They can use their knowledge to be quite tyrannical. In the third decan, they achieve a bigger viewpoint, considering the happiness of others, and become model citizens, and often role models for others. They can still manipulate easily, but use it for common good, rather than selfishly.

Qabalah

Earlier, the role of Qabalah as the Tree of Life, was dismissed as a meaning for the Major arcana, due to chronological reasons. i.e. the Tarot came first, then Kircher’s tree structure.

However, the same issue is not present for the Minor Arcana. The Golden Dawn came well after Kircher’s tree, and its members were well versed in it. The Golden Dawn, and hence both Crowley and Waite, seem to have used both astrology and the sefira in designing their cards. The Elements in the Minor Arcana

In the Major Arcana, there are two different versions of the elements. The higher elements issue forth from the cloud in[P6], but then mother Nature makes her own versions, as per

[P12] In the Mind is the Archetypal Form, which has always been and is limitless. Nature received the Word and copied the Archetypal world, becoming her own cosmos, by use of her own elements and the births of souls.

Thus, we have two forms of the elements, the Archetypal and the Natural. The Archetypal are shown in Waite’s Wheel of Fortune having wings and books, amongst the clouds, and the Natural are shown in his World card without wings and books.

The four symbols of the elements are:

● Fire: Lion
● Air: Eagle
● Water: Human or Angel
● Earth: Bull.

It is interesting that Water is not represented by a fish.

The four elements are generally considered to have originated at least as far back as ancient Sumeria (Mesopotamia). In their most physical form, they are simply plasma, gas, liquid and solid. However, the Minor takes the Pymander philosophy that the below is governed by the above, and so the affairs of man are largely determined by the Governors, and the archetypal elements, as indicated in the Wheel of Fortune.

Thus, the minor cards are more to do with the nature of the elements as expressed through Astrology, rather than mere physical phases of matter. This book’s attributions of meaning are:

Fire = Wands

Action. Creation. Doing. Change. Will.
Wands indicate the energy of living plants, the energy of burning wood. Fire imbues in people the ability to understand how the world works, as in the processes of change, and to make real change in the world. Despite the usual association of Fire with physical action, in reality it seems to be more to do with wilfulness.

Air = Swords

Social Interaction, Society, Justice

Swords represent the force that lies behind social governance, and the aggressive side of social interaction.

Air imbues in people the ability to understand human interactions.

Water = Cups

Emotions. Feelings.

Cups represent wine, the blood of Christ, the Holy Grail, religion.

Water imbues in people the ability to understand emotions.

Earth = Pentacles

Material wealth, Comfort, Security

Pentacles represent coin money that is used to trade physical goods and services.

Earth imbues in people the ability to understand the practical needs of survival of the individual and of the species. Also the physical abilities of Strength and constitution.

Decan Descriptions

The astrological influences used in this book have been drawn from a variety of sources, including Ibn Ezra, Picatrix, and Agrippa, Golden Dawn, A.E. Waite, but primarily modernised through use of Gary Goldschneider’s Personology, astrological properties of the decans, and the RWS pip cards themselves. Goldschneider has been paraphrased in this book.

Decans appear to be undergoing a resurgence at this time, and the interested reader can easily find numerous astrology sites on the web featuring information by Decan.

Most of the early decan background material has been moved into Appendix D, so that readers may decide for themselves regarding consistency and clarity of meaning.

According to imagery, it appears that the Waite Smith deck follows the Golden Dawn Book-T meanings, and that Gary Goldschneider was influenced by RWS drawings, using words that accurately described the RWS pictures. For example, the RWS Ten of Cups shows a scene of a happy family dream come true, with children dancing, and Goldschneider calls it the week of Dancers and Dreamers. Such visual to literal correspondences appear in several places.

The real value of using Goldschneider lies in the fact the he amassed a personal history of 14,000 individual horoscopes, along with interviews with the subjects. Thus, by using his work, we have a consistent lineage of Decans from Ibn Ezra, Picatrix, Agrippa, Golden Dawn, Waite and Coleman Smith to Goldschneider, and his probable validation of the originals against modern research techniques.

Waite’s Key to the Tarot. The material from Waite himself has been considered, and there is an admitted temptation to simply accept his words as gospel, given that he and Pamela Coleman-Smith designed the cards. However, it is clear from Waite’s writings that what he has assembled is an overview of common divinatory meanings, and not necessarily his own deductions. There is some evidence of the Book-T influence in his Key to the Tarot. Lastly, there is Waite’s self-confessed and deliberate obscurity. Taking all of this into consideration, rather than simply regurgitate Waite’s words, this document takes a view that the Decan approach will yield the correct answer. It does so with the added value of Goldschneider’s astrology, which was not available to Waite, and which seems to have been developed using a more experiential approach than the earlier decan descriptions which seem more theoretical.

In general, the Pymander meanings chosen in this book lean towards Decans, as (nearly) described by Goldschneider, but with an eye to preserving consistency with Coleman-Smith imagery.

Card Images

In this book, three cards are presented for each – an original Waite-Smith, and one from the Steele Wizard deck, which was strongly influenced by the Waite deck., and one created by the author. There is little point in showing others such as Thoth, or Tarot de Marseilles, as these do not show explanatory scenes.
